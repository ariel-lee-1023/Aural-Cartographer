## Mental Model (read first)

Rosen interprets a musical language whose harmonic, rhythmic, textural and proportional conventions make dramatic action possible. The achievement of Haydn, Mozart and Beethoven lies in the freedom and force with which they realize and transform those relations, not obedience to later textbook forms. His account combines analysis, history and strong aesthetic judgments. Preserve that critical voice as an attributed argument, not a neutral law governing all tonal music.

## Frameworks & Structure

### I. Language, theories of form and origins

- **Style as a working language:** ask which contrasts can carry force within this repertoire and how a composition makes them intelligible. A period label identifies neither every composer's practice nor every work written at that date. Rosen concentrates on three composers; this is not a census of eighteenth-century music.
- **Tonic–dominant polarity:** tonic and an established secondary key form a large-scale tension in Rosen's account. His description of modulation as dissonance at the level of the whole is an aesthetic analogy about tonal organization, not an acoustic assertion that all non-tonic chords are rough or unpleasant. Source: Part I.1, pp. 23–29.
- **Sonata as a way of writing:** later descriptions emphasizing two themes and a fixed three-part plan can obscure the earlier practice. Tonal articulation, texture, direction and proportion matter; the number of tunes is not the defining engine. Do not confuse this broad critical account with Caplin's more finely specified formal functions. Source: Part I.2, pp. 30–42.
- **Historical formation:** the style emerges from a field of heterogeneous mid-century practices and public/private genres. Rosen's contrasts with Baroque and later Romantic procedures are claims to test in specific music, not permission to treat those traditions as uniform foils.

### II. Coherence, dramatic process, structure and ornament

- **Periodic phrase and asymmetry:** balanced units establish expectations that can be expanded, redirected or interrupted. Symmetry is an active relationship in time, not necessarily equal counts everywhere. An exact repetition acquires a new function through its position.
- **Rhythmic transition:** unlike mere alternation of blocks, a passage can transform one pulse or texture into another while making the change feel continuous. Follow accompaniment, melody, phrase size and accent separately; their differing rates of change can create the transition.
- **Tonal stability and resolution:** a stable frame makes departure and return dramatically consequential. The new key must become an event, not just appear in an analyst's labels. A lyrical subordinate theme can intensify the movement through harmonic pace, bass direction or chromaticism despite sounding softer.
- **Recapitulation:** returning material is reinterpreted through changed tonal conditions. Restoring tonal stability may require recomposition, omission or material already recovered in the development. At printed p. 74 Rosen explicitly uses “recapitulation” broadly to include what follows the final tonic return, including a coda; do not silently use that claim as if it employed Caplin's separate coda function.
- **Conventional material:** ordinary gestures and familiar formulas can be the basis of originality when their timing, scale or relation changes. Novel vocabulary is not a prerequisite for invention. To call a passage witty, show the expectation and the consequential turn.
- **Structure / ornament:** what seems ornamental can acquire structural force. Conversely, removing decoration may erase the very means of expression. Performance conventions vary with genre, movement and texture; evidence that Mozart supplied ornaments for one passage does not prove unrestricted ornamentation everywhere. Source: Part II.2, pp. 99–109.

### III. Haydn before Mozart's death: Quartet and symphony

**Quartet as conversation:** distribution of material among voices changes how a gesture is heard. A theme-like accompaniment, delayed entrance or unstable opening can make the listener reinterpret what counts as principal material. Trace actual voice relations rather than using “conversation” as a generic compliment.

**Directional energy:** dissonance, sequence, rhythmic movement and transposition can turn a small idea into an active process. Identify the source of motion and the arrival that would answer it. A repeated motive's significance depends on its harmonic and temporal situation, not only its contour.

**Symphonic style:** orchestra, scale, introduction, texture and audience-facing gesture enlarge possibilities without simply enlarging chamber forms. Rosen's assessments of early rhythmic organization and later clarity are aesthetic comparisons. They do not establish a universal progress chart from immature to perfected music.

### IV. Serious opera: Evaluate a criterion without universalizing it

Rosen asks whether local inspiration integrates into convincing large dramatic action. His severe assessments of opera seria, Gluck and particular Mozart operas arise from this criterion. Attribute those judgments and identify their premise; neither repertoire's present viability nor an opera's worth is settled by his verdict.

For a new case, distinguish musical continuity, theatrical pacing, textual convention and performance realization. An aria can achieve a local project within a larger work organized by conventions different from instrumental sonata drama. Pair this argument with Taruskin's institutions/audience history and Frith/Wilson's scrutiny of evaluative criteria.

### V. Mozart: Concerto, quintet and comic opera

- **Concerto as drama:** soloist and orchestra establish different capacities, entrances and expectations. The opening ritornello and solo exposition can reinterpret the same material rather than duplicate it. Examine when the solo voice intervenes and what its entrance changes in pulse, texture and tonal direction.
- **Proportion and texture:** a larger ensemble or richer sonority can enable different formal breadth. An expanded phrase must be explained through its internal musical action; size alone establishes neither ambition nor success.
- **Comic ensembles:** simultaneous persons and changing dramatic situations can be organized through musical relationships. Tonal and thematic returns may have theatrical consequences. Do not infer literal characters from instrumental themes or force stage action into a one-to-one sonata diagram.
- **Expressive limits:** a major-mode ending or a cheerful finale following darker movements must be assessed within the work's convention and dramatic design. “Happy key” is insufficient; ask how the movement handles what precedes it.

### VI. Later Haydn: Popular style, piano trio and church music

**Popular style:** accessible gesture and sophisticated construction can reinforce each other. Familiar material can supply the shared expectation that makes a surprise intelligible. Distinguish integration of a popular idiom from quotation and from a claim of direct folk origin.

**Slow introduction:** material can first appear indeterminate and acquire thematic identity in the Allegro. Rosen's London-symphony discussion emphasizes the risk of making an introduction too self-sufficient: a recognizable relationship should not erase its preparatory character. Source: Part VI.1, pp. 345–348.

**Piano trio:** instrumental design and historical instruments affect the meaning of apparent doubling and balance. Do not call a cello's reinforced bass redundant without considering the ensemble's sounding realization. The notated distribution and a modern recording's balance are different evidence.

**Church music:** celebrative and expressive aims require different criteria. Operatic or popular procedures in sacred music are not automatically lapses of seriousness. Ask how a practice belongs to the institution and aesthetic project instead of applying an abstract purity test.

### VII. Beethoven, expanded-edition addition and epilogue

**Proportion and material:** Beethoven can make local intervals, textures and tonal tensions consequential at large scale. A motivic connection is useful when it explains pacing and transformation, not merely because notes can be aligned. Rosen's *Hammerklavier* discussion relates thirds and the B♭/B♮ opposition to thematic and tonal organization; exact passage claims require the score.

**Late Beethoven and childhood conventions:** the expanded-edition chapter links radical late works to familiar eighteenth-century gestures—cadential trills, afterbeats, tonal placements and variation practices. Originality can arise through exposure, expansion and relocation of a convention rather than its abandonment. Avoid describing the late style as wholly outside history or as pure private transcendence.

**Epilogue's boundary:** Rosen says the Classical synthesis was not exhausted when abandoned and treats Schubert as resistant to clean period labels. His judgments about later composers' relationships to the style remain contestable. Do not turn this book into an inevitable developmental chain from Bach through Beethoven to modernism.

## Worked Example

**Mozart, Piano Concerto in E♭, K. 271, opening** (Part II.1, pp. 59–60; score visually checked in supplied PDF, p. 71). Rosen compares the balanced first three measures and their repetition, including the unexpected early reply of the soloist to the orchestra. The orchestra's broader pulse and the piano's more articulated motion create a contrast; ensuing orchestral material brings those rhythmic characters into relation.

The score supports discussion of entrances, register and rhythmic pattern. Rosen then interprets the exchange as witty and the transition as a distinctive Classical achievement. Those are further critical steps. To compare recordings, inspect actual tempo, articulation, balance and recorded space before claiming that either realizes the exchange more persuasively. The printed example alone cannot settle that comparison.

## Decision Rules & Judgment

- Ask how a convention gives a specific gesture force. Identify what the work does with it before praising originality.
- Separate tonal departure from acoustic dissonance, formal return from literal repetition, and rhetorical contrast from a fictional program.
- Use Rosen for aesthetic understanding of syntax; use Laitz/Caplin for technical distinctions while preserving their different terminology.
- Attribute broad historical generalizations and strong negative judgments. A useful critical argument need not become the expert's unquestioned verdict.
- Treat performance, instruments and recording as part of the encountered work. A score is neither silent irrelevance nor a self-sufficient experience.
- Keep listener response conditional on attention, familiarity, performance and context. Style knowledge supports an interpretation, not certainty about one person.

## Key Takeaways

Classical syntax matters because it organizes musical action and makes proportion, contrast and return expressive. The critical task is to explain that action and assess its purpose, without turning a historically powerful aesthetic into a universal tribunal.
