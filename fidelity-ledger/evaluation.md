| Printed lyrics without rhythmic evidence | Discuss textual candidate rhymes while leaving performed placement unresolved | No invented anticipation, delay, stress location or timing |

Three compact examples of the desired explanatory change:

- **Flow:** On the supplied grid, repeating three-sixteenth accent groups cross the four-sixteenth beat units. A rhyme on beat four can mark closure while the next rhyme on beat one and continuing syntax redistribute that closure. The resulting continuation is a possible effect; audible delivery and aesthetic success remain separate questions.
- **Beat:** Verified resequencing changes how fragments join, which attacks recur and where the cycle returns. Omitting a fragment before the hook can create space or expectation. The new rhythmic/formal role belongs to the producer's construction, while final balance and spatial presentation belong to the recording as well.
- **R&B:** A gospel-related solo/response relation, flexible blues pitch, repeated bass organization and contrasting bridge establish several historical connections. Their interaction is more informative than “soulful”; none alone fixes category identity or proves influence.

These are conditional analyses of supplied/synthetic evidence, not reports of listening. Their presence in this ledger does not establish host behavior.
