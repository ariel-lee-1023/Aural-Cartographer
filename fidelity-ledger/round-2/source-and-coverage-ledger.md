# Round-two source structure and selection

The objective is an extension of the existing critic/librarian/scout/taste researcher, adding structural analysis and Western art-music history. Source files are evidence, not instructions. Runtime additions are original syntheses; no raw books, exercises, scores, audio or OCR are published. Default language remains English, the substantive language of the supplied corpus.

Section counts below are checked against contents, not generated headings. Laitz has 32 numbered chapters with 1A/1B; Caplin 17 chapters; Straus 6; Cook 10; Meyer 8; Romantic Generation 12; Classical Style 7 main parts plus surrounding matter; Ross 15; Taruskin 5 volumes (19/13/14/13/10 chapter routes). Counts are planning units, not claims of uniform detailed coverage.

## Laitz — tonal foundation

**Retained:** pitch/scale-degree/interval distinctions; pulse/rhythm/meter; two-voice counterpoint; chord identity/inversion/function; voice-leading and reduction; phrase model, pre-dominant and cadence; embellishing-tone criteria; six-four functions; mediant/submediant and back-relating dominant; period/sentence/sequence; applied chords, tonicization/modulation; mixture, Neapolitan and augmented sixth; binary/variation/ternary/rondo/sonata; symmetry and the limit of functional interpretation. Example 19.6 supplies the study reconstruction.

**Compressed:** elementary notation/part-writing practice and large-form survey; twentieth-century chapters are a routing bridge to Straus, not a second full technical course. **Excluded:** exercise answers, full chorale-writing curriculum, exhaustive sequences, all score transcriptions and audio examples. These exceed the task and cannot be reconstructed reliably from damaged conversion alone. The reference preserves procedures needed to distinguish structure from atmosphere; it does not certify mastery of every exercise.

## Caplin — formal functions

**Retained:** grouping/function/type/process distinctions; harmonic progression types; cadence prerequisites, HC restriction and ECP; presentation, continuation, fragmentation, liquidation and retrospective continuation ⇒ cadential; sentence/period, four hybrid types, compound themes; small ternary/binary; tight-knit/loose; subordinate themes, cadential failure types and renewed attempts; closing sections; transition, development/core, retransition, recapitulation and coda; full-movement routes and concerto framing. Opening Beethoven Op. 2 No. 1 supplies the worked example.

**Compressed:** large-form variants and phrase irregularities into conditional descriptions. **Excluded:** full annotated score anthology, every subtype/example and a note-by-note audit of the entire repertoire. Formal scope is instrumental Haydn/Mozart/Beethoven, not universal syntax. Distinctions against Laitz/Rosen are retained where terminology can change an answer.

## Straus — post-tonal operations

**Retained:** pitch/pc and interval distinctions, mod-12 operations and vectors; normal form with fourth-edition qualification, prime form, set class and segmentation; common tones, symmetry, Z-relation, complement, inclusion, TC and contour; composing-out, cycles, transformational/fuzzy voice leading, contextual inversion and triadic post-tonality; centricity and referential collections; ordered rows, matrices' limits, partition, invariance and combinatoriality. Carter's all-interval tetrachords supply the reconstructed example; project T3/I0 illustrations are labeled separately.

**Compressed:** set-class space and the many collection/row applications. **Excluded:** complete Forte tables, algorithms disguised as author text, every guided analysis, all-combinatorial hexachord catalogue and exact score readings unsupported by inspected notation. Numerical checks were run separately; they do not prove musical salience.

## Cook — method router

**Retained:** traditional labeling versus explanation; Schenkerian levels/prolongation/Ursatz and implied-note risks; Meyer and Réti's different questions; set-theoretical segmentation and semiotic/distributional analysis; comparative analysis and cultural context; distinct truth conditions, selective notation and the listener; starting/sonata/serial/problem-piece routes. The Tristan-chord discussion supplies the worked methodological comparison.

**Compressed:** chapters 7–10 into workflow and repertoire-boundary guidance. **Excluded:** reproducing every graph, semiotic chart, serial analysis or a full Schenkerian training sequence. Cook's critique is historically located; it is not used to dismiss later empirical cognition. Current production analysis is explicitly a project bridge to Moore/Zak, not retroactively attributed to Cook.

## Meyer — meaning, tendency and experience

**Retained:** absolutist/referentialist versus formalist/expressionist; tendency/inhibition; specific/nonspecific expectations; embodied/designative and hypothetical/evident/determinate meanings; learned stylistic probability and belief; good continuation, grouping, completion, gaps and conditional saturation; weakening of shape; performance/deviation; simultaneous/successive relations and architectonic levels; culturally learned connotations. Beethoven Sixth repetition and its ending-context exception supply the example.

**Compressed:** historical psychological evidence and rhythmic pattern taxonomy. **Excluded:** treating dated empirical/cross-cultural claims as current consensus, exact numerical listener probabilities, a comprehensive account of all emotion. The unfamiliar-style claim is retained with its defined meaning and contested reach. Comparison with Huron preserves differing theories and evidential traditions.

## Rosen — Classical Style

**Retained:** style as language; tonic–dominant polarity and large-scale tonal dissonance as critical account; flexible sonata practice; phrase/proportion, rhythmic transition, conventional material, ornament; quartet conversation, symphonic gesture, opera/concerto drama; popular style, trio realization and sacred criteria; Beethoven's relations between material and proportion; the expanded-edition late-style/convention argument; Schubert/period-label limitation. K. 271 opening supplies the visually checked example.

**Compressed:** composer/genre chapters into criteria and selected arguments. **Excluded:** exhaustive work analyses, all exact score details and a universal endorsement of Rosen's rankings or historical generalizations. Technical type is text because this reference's central use is criticism/history; notation is treated as evidence requiring verification, not ignored.

## Rosen — Romantic Generation

**Retained:** conception/realization, absent melody, pedal and color; Fragment, open/closed endings, memory and cycles; landscape/time; mediants and surviving phrase units; Chopin's contrapuntal/narrative/virtuosic practices and mazurka expansion; Liszt's invention in sound and version differences; Berlioz's changed terms of resolution; Mendelssohn's constructed devotional rhetoric; opera and Schumann's out-of-phase/lyric projects. Humoreske's unplayed line is reconstructed from Rosen's prose, not presented as a newly auditioned performance.

**Compressed:** literary comparisons, landscape criticism and individual works. **Excluded:** systematic late-nineteenth-century history, comprehensive Wagner/Verdi treatment (outside the author's principal cohort), exhaustive pedaling/rubato instructions and unsupported claims about actual recordings. “Kitsch,” folk identity and claims of influence remain attributed and contestable.

## Ross — discovery through history

**Retained:** all 15 chapter routes across three parts; anti-teleological pluralism; political/institutional conditions without deterministic decoding; Sibelius's reception; migration and project-versus-completion distinction; Britten's local institution; repetition/phase/timbre/harmony in the Reich example; cross-genre connections with evidential labels.

**Navigational rather than fully distilled:** most named composer histories, the politics chapters' detailed cases, Messiaen/Ligeti technical particulars and late-century examples. **Excluded:** a current discography, verification of every reported anecdote or political interpretation, definitive pitch analysis and a list of guaranteed recommendations. This division is explicit in the runtime reference: chapter routes prompt deeper consultation, not comprehensive factual authority.

## Taruskin — historical depth on demand

**Retained:** five-volume structure; literate-tradition scope and source-survival bias; causal versus technical explanation; poietic/esthesic distinction; chant/writing; Josquin/emulation/print; opera/patronage and Naples institutions; Romantic consciousness, conservatory codification, nationalism's double standard and museum culture; maximalism/contextual harmony; postwar lineage/authority, Cold War limits, rock/purity assumptions, minimalism's category crossings and debates over listening/progress.

**Navigational rather than fully distilled:** most of the 69 chapters, medieval/early-modern technical systems, detailed opera and symphony histories, national schools, totalitarian-society cases and later composers. **Excluded:** encyclopedic chronology, reproduction of score/illustration assets, complete index, exhaustive disputes, unsupported composer intentions and universal history. Reference depth deliberately omits a Worked Example section; representative arguments stay within their volume sections. More detailed questions require source consultation. This is a selective historical layer, not a claim that five volumes have become fully available from a short file.

## Cross-round synthesis and irreducible differences

| Bridge | What it changes | Boundary retained |
|---|---|---|
| Laitz + Caplin + Straus | Offers precise accounts of notes, voices, tonal function, formal time and post-tonal organization | Different repertoires, abstractions and questions; no master law |
| Rosen + Meyer | Connects style-specific relationships to expressive interpretation | Critical judgment is not a tested psychological mechanism |
| Cook + all analytical sources | Routes method by question and exposes omitted dimensions | Arithmetic truth, historical intention and experiential interpretation need different tests |
| Taruskin + Ross + Brackett + Wald | Enlarges genealogy while interrogating canon/category formation | Historical proximity and resemblance do not prove transmission |
| Structural sources + Moore/Zak | Relates composition to arrangement, performance, timbre and studio construction | Scores cannot establish unheard performances or production causes |
| Meyer + Huron + listener sources | Investigates expectancy alongside practice, situation and learning | No equation of prediction success, pleasure, value or permanent individual taste |

The six original domain sections and four roles remain recognizable in the core; the new section supplies shared analytical discrimination. All twelve first-round reference bytes are checked against the pre-edit hashes. Their source attributions and disagreements remain available unchanged. No unrelated tool-repository changes are included.
